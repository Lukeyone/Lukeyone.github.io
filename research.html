Independent research · 2026

Peak phosphorus and food-system resilience

Format25-page technical report
MethodLiterature synthesis and expert interviews
StatusPortfolio research—not peer reviewed

The central conclusion is not that the world will literally run out of phosphorus on a fixed date. The more defensible risk is the loss of cheap, clean and politically accessible phosphate—combined with processing, energy, water, logistics and governance constraints.

01

Resources are not access

Large geological resources can coexist with constrained affordable supply when ore quality, contaminants, capital, energy, water and processing capacity are limiting.

02

Scarcity would be unequal

Regions with legacy soil phosphorus, capital and strong institutions have buffers that poorer import-dependent countries often lack.

03

Industrial dependencies matter

Phosphate rock alone is insufficient; fertiliser supply also relies on acids, ammonia, electricity, water, transport, ports, finance and political stability.

04

Adaptation is real but slow

Precision application, recycling, erosion control and legacy-soil drawdown can reduce demand, but infrastructure and adoption cannot be created instantly during a crisis.

Evidence discipline

Separating forecast, observation and scenario inference

The report traces the origin of the widely repeated 2070 peak-production estimate, contrasts it with larger technical reserve-base assessments, and labels geopolitical scenarios as inference rather than scientific prediction. International doctoral researchers were contacted to challenge technical assumptions and identify remaining evidence gaps.

01

PyTorch · Computer vision

Agricultural pests, recyclable waste and domain shift

Problem

Build multiclass image classifiers and test how performance changes when images come from a less controlled source.

Method

Custom CNNs, convolution and pooling, batch normalisation, dropout, global-average pooling, Adam and SGD, TensorBoard, PyTorch Profiler, weighted sampling, confusion matrices and per-class analysis. A separate ResNet-18 experiment used CIFAR-10 coarse labels.

Recorded evidence

The strongest documented pest run reached 82.81% test accuracy and approximately 0.83 weighted F1. The waste model recorded 70.30% test accuracy and 0.70 weighted F1 in-domain, then approximately 50% accuracy on independently sourced images.

Limitations and lesson

One later experimental model attached its optimiser to the wrong parameter set and is excluded from promoted results. The out-of-domain decline reinforced that a clean held-out split is not equivalent to real-world robustness.

02

Geological NLP · Domain adaptation

Geological language-model adaptation benchmark

Problem

Test whether full fine-tuning or parameter-efficient LoRA could improve GPT-2 on held-out Australian geological text without degrading related or out-of-domain performance.

Method

Document-separated Geoscience Australia sources, 512-token training chunks, token-weighted sliding-window perplexity, pretrained/full/LoRA comparisons, an out-of-domain parliamentary control, training-curve analysis and fixed-prompt qualitative review.

Recorded evidence

Pretrained GPT-2 achieved 24.645 validation perplexity. Full fine-tuning worsened this to 25.279 while gaining 1.80% on related geology and degrading out-of-domain perplexity by 5.74%. LoRA reached 24.856 validation perplexity while using 98.7% fewer trainable parameters, 63.6% less runtime and 62.5% less peak GPU memory.

Limitations and lesson

Neither adaptation method improved held-out geological validation performance, both began overfitting early, and generated text remained scientifically unreliable. The defensible result is a negative benchmark that redirects the next iteration toward grounded evidence extraction, source citations and explicit abstention.

03

Environmental data · Unsupervised learning

Microclimate sensor clustering

Problem

Determine whether environmental sensor observations contain stable natural groupings without imposing a target label.

Method

65,119 observations, missing-value imputation, feature standardisation, PCA retaining 95% of variance, K-means, DBSCAN, agglomerative clustering, silhouette scoring and VAT/iVAT visual assessment.

Recorded evidence

Approximate silhouette scores were 0.226 for K-means, 0.165 for agglomerative clustering and −0.516 for DBSCAN.

Limitations and lesson

The main result is negative: the tested representation did not reveal strongly separated clusters. That outcome was reported rather than reframed as success, and it motivates better temporal, spatial and domain-informed features.

04

Scientific tabular ML · Data reconciliation

Australian food classification

Problem

Predict broad hierarchical food categories from Australian food-composition measurements assembled from sources with incompatible bases and extensive missingness.

Method

Source-table reconciliation, exclusion of incompatible per-100 mL records, high-missingness and constant-feature removal, outlier treatment, label consolidation, MinMax scaling, decision trees, random forests, GridSearchCV and separate train, validation and test sets.

Recorded evidence

The strongest recorded configurations reached approximately 87–89% test accuracy, with class-level error and overfitting analysis.

Limitations and lesson

The most important decision was not the model: it was refusing to combine scientifically incompatible measurement bases. Further work would use grouped cross-validation, calibrated probabilities and clearer hierarchical metrics.

05

Geospatial geometry · Spatial reasoning

Coordinate systems, polygons and representative regions

Problem

Aggregate multiple geographic inputs into a representative region while handling country geometry, coordinate systems and complex polygon boundaries.

Method

GeoPandas, Shapely, GeoJSON, CRS transformation, boundary resampling, polygon operations, convex hulls and custom plotting functions.

Recorded evidence

The notebook created and visualised an aggregated region from multiple geographic inputs, demonstrating custom spatial geometry rather than point-only mapping.

Limitations and lesson

The construction is exploratory and should not be interpreted as a statistically validated population model. Its value is evidence of spatial-data preparation and geometric reasoning.

Curation method

Why the raw archive is not public.

The original archive contains drafts, exact duplicates, empty notebooks, Drive-specific paths, personal data and at least one historical credential. Publishing everything would be less transparent—not more—because it would mix valid evidence with unsafe or methodologically weak material.

  • Exact duplicates are represented once.
  • Iterations are consolidated under the strongest defensible version.
  • Known bugs and nonstandard metrics are disclosed.
  • Credentials, applicant information and restricted data are never reproduced.
  • Raw notebooks will be published only after sanitisation and reproducibility work.
